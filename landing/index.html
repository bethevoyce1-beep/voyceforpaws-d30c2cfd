What this is

Every day, public shelters publish lists of animals at risk of euthanasia. Voyce takes those lists and turns every animal into a shareable card — with their real photo, real notes, and the exact deadline. Rescues, fosters, and adopters get alerted in time to act.

We're starting with San Antonio ACS because their daily at-risk list is public — no partnership needed. Every other shelter that publishes a list is next.

❓ Frequently Asked

Questions, answered honestly.

Tap any question to expand.

Is Voyce free? +
Yes. Always. Voyce is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. No fees for reporting an animal, joining the community network, or receiving alerts. Ever.
When does Voyce launch? +
Voyce launches in Ohio first this summer, then rolls out across every U.S. state, one by one. Join the waitlist to be notified the moment we go live in your area.
Who can join the community network? +
Anyone who cares. Animal lovers, rescuers, fosters, shelters, transporters, volunteers, and everyday neighbors. You don’t need credentials to help — you just need to want to.
What if there’s no one nearby? +
Voyce ripples the alert outward. Closest first, then wider until help is found. Shelters and rescues farther out get notified too, so distance doesn’t mean delay.
Is my data private? +
Yes. We never sell your data. Your information is only used to coordinate help for animals.
I’m not a rescuer. Can I still help? +
Absolutely. Most of the community network is everyday people. Spotting an animal in trouble, taking one photo, sharing a foster space, or driving 10 minutes to transport — that’s the community network.
Does Voyce actually rescue the animal? +
Voyce is the alert system. The community network does the rescue. We connect the moment you see an animal to the people who can help — shelters, rescuers, fosters, and animal lovers in your area.
How is Voyce different from posting on social media? +
Social posts get buried in feeds and reach whoever happens to be scrolling. Voyce goes directly to the people who can act — verified shelters, rescuers, fosters, and responders in range — in real time.
